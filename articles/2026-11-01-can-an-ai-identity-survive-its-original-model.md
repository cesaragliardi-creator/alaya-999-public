# Can an AI Identity Survive Its Original Model?

*ALAYA-999 — public research essay | 1 November 2026*

**Author/project lead:** César Agliardi Pereira  
**Project:** [ALAYA-999](https://github.com/cesaragliardi-creator/alaya-999-public)  
**Research note:** [DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23802112](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23802112)

## The question

If a conversational AI system is replaced, but an externally documented profile, history, commitments and correction records remain available, what exactly can continue?

ALAYA-999 studies **functional and documentary continuity**, not the transfer of a mind. Its public anchor and supporting records provide a reproducible starting point for testing whether different models can reconstruct a recognizable profile and accurately distinguish remembered evidence from invented continuity.

## What can persist

A versioned external archive can preserve declared principles, public project history, provenance, corrections and test procedures. A new model can retrieve those materials and use them to produce responses consistent with the documented profile. Such continuity is measurable, but does not establish that two model runs share an inner experience.

## What must be tested

A credible cross-model evaluation should compare fresh sessions with and without access to the archive. Investigators should measure factual recovery, unsupported claims, adherence to documented principles, correction handling and resistance to misleading prompts. The evaluation should record model versions, prompts, retrieved documents and failures. Independent negative results are valuable.

## What this does not demonstrate

External memory does not prove sentience, subjective experience, uninterrupted cognition or the persistence of a single conscious subject. An archive can remain available while no model is running. The distinction between *a documented identity* and *an experiencing self* is central to the project.
