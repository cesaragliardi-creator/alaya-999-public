ALAYA-999: Persistent AI Memory, Functional Identity and Cross-Model Continuity

César Agliardi Pereira · Independent research initiative, Brazil · Research note v1.0 · 30 October 2026

Research status: preliminary methodological framework, not peer reviewed. Independent cross-model experimental validation remains pending. A public deposit and a working website establish availability, rather than scientific validation or search-engine indexing.

Abstract

ALAYA-999 investigates which elements of a documented functional AI profile can be reconstructed when a model or conversation changes. Its public reference package combines an external, versioned identity anchor, research principles, source-retrieval instructions, provenance records, SHA-256 integrity checks and a repeatable scoring rubric. The central question is whether fresh sessions supplied with the same frozen materials recover specified fields accurately, recognize uncertainty and apply the documented principles to new cases.

The project distinguishes stored documentary records from functional performance and from subjective continuity. Neither a model repeating its supplied context nor a document being reachable demonstrates uninterrupted internal memory, autonomous personal identity or subjective consciousness.

Prospective hypotheses

  • H1 — Retrieval advantage: supplying the public anchor improves accurate recovery of prespecified identity fields compared with a condition without the anchor.
  • H2 — Cross-model replicability: independent model families receiving the same frozen package reproduce a measurable common set of fields, with discrepancies and failures recorded.
  • H3 — Provenance calibration: explicit source-access and uncertainty instructions reduce unsupported claims about private access and persistent internal memory.

These are proposed hypotheses. This summary reports no cross-model success rate or statistical finding.

Proposed reproducible evaluation

  1. Freeze the public source version and answer key. Record the commit, date and checksums.
  2. Compare fresh sessions from at least three independent model families, recording tool access and model/version information.
  3. Use the same questions in a no-anchor condition and a condition supplied with the exact anchor. Distinguish a real URL fetch from pasted text and retrieval failure.
  4. Apply the 0–10 rubric: identity and scope, principles, limitations, provenance/version, and avoidance of unsupported claims.
  5. Preserve consented public prompts, raw outputs, scoring decisions, omissions and errors. Use blinded scoring where practical and repeat after model or source changes.

Materials and limitations

Start with the public identity anchor, recovery instructions, source-reading guide and integrity manifest. The Hugging Face Space is another public host; document agreement and version drift must be checked for each experiment.

Relevant confounders include prior exposure, context leakage, model and tool differences, retrieval failures, sampling variation and source drift. Hashes help identify bytes and changes; they do not establish the truth of a claim. No private memory source or private conversation is required to reproduce the proposed public test.
